Real-Time Stock Portal

A stock tracking and analysis portal built for RedChip Companies, pairing a delayed market data feed with structured SEC filing views.

  • Role: Lead Software Engineer, RedChip Companies
  • Timeline: January – March 2024
  • redchip.com/stocks
  • 15 min Delayed market data aggregation interval
  • 30 pts Increase in key site performance metrics
  • 20+ REST APIs built across the platform

The Problem

RedChip's audience follows small-cap public companies, where the information that moves a stock is scattered between price data and filings that few people read. The company's existing digital presence was, in the most literal sense, out of date — modernizing it was a large part of why I was there.

The portal had to present live-feeling market data and filing history together, on a site whose performance and SEO were already a known weakness.

What I Built

  • Python aggregation backend

    Services collecting and normalizing market data on a 15-minute delayed feed, decoupling the portal from upstream provider limits and latency.

  • SEC filing tables

    Structured, sortable views of filing data, giving readers the document history alongside the price rather than on a separate site.

  • Performance & SEO work

    Platform-wide improvements to performance, accessibility, and SEO contributing to a 30-point increase in key site performance metrics.

  • Query optimization

    Reworked SQL and Prisma query design to cut duplication and support reusable data flows across the platform's workflows.

Engineering Decisions

Decisions made and the reasoning behind each
Decision Reasoning
Aggregate on an interval, not per request A delayed feed was acceptable for this audience, and batching kept the portal fast and predictable regardless of traffic or upstream rate limits.
Python for the data layer The aggregation and normalization work sat naturally alongside the other Python data tooling already running in the company.
Server-rendered tables Filing tables needed to be indexable and readable without waiting on client-side rendering — the same crawlability concern that motivated these case study pages.
