Promo — Event-Based Social Mobile App

A cross-platform app for discovering exclusive local business promotions, built at Z-Axis Solutions on a Node.js and MongoDB backend.

  • Role: Jr Full Stack Developer, Z-Axis Solutions
  • Timeline: August 2022 – September 2023
  • Platforms: iOS, Android
  • 40+ Backend API endpoints expanded and maintained
  • 20+ Features delivered across production apps
  • 5 Engineers coordinated with on delivery

The Problem

Promo connected local businesses running limited-time offers with people nearby who would act on them. That makes it an event-driven product: the content is time-bound, the audience is location-bound, and a promotion that surfaces late is worthless.

The backend had to serve that freshness reliably to mobile clients while the team kept adding surface area — chat, email automation, exports — without destabilizing what already shipped.

What I Built

  • REST API surface

    Expanded and maintained 40+ backend endpoints in Node.js and Express, improving reliability and delivery of database-driven content.

  • Real-time chat

    One of 20+ features delivered, letting users and businesses communicate inside the app rather than through an external channel.

  • Email automation

    Middleware and automated email workflows handling lifecycle messaging without manual sends.

  • Data export systems

    Export tooling so businesses could pull their own promotion performance data out of the platform.

Practices

This was the role where testing discipline became habit rather than instruction. I applied TDD with Jest and Postman across the API surface, which measurably improved reliability and cut production defects — on a codebase where 40+ endpoints shared models and a careless change could break something three features away.

I also mentored interns on debugging, accessibility standards, and engineering practice, and coordinated with a team of 5 engineers to deliver a full educational event platform spanning backend, frontend, and infrastructure.

Writing tests first on a shared API is less about correctness in the moment than about making the next person's change safe. That is the same instinct that later turned into evaluation infrastructure for LLM systems — build the harness so a regression announces itself.
