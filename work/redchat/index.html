RedChat — AI SEC Filing Summarizer

A retrieval-augmented chatbot that turns dense SEC filings into quick, sourced answers — covering every company listed on the NYSE and NASDAQ.

  • Role: Lead Software Engineer, RedChip Companies
  • Timeline: January 2024 – January 2025
  • red.chat
  • 100,000+ Filings loaded and prepared for retrieval
  • 1,000+ Documents indexed in the AWS RAG system
  • 3 Platforms shipped: web, iOS, Android

The Problem

SEC filings are public, comprehensive, and nearly unreadable at speed. A 10-K runs hundreds of pages of boilerplate wrapped around a handful of facts an investor actually wants. RedChip's audience — investors researching small-cap public companies — needed those facts without reading the document, and needed to trust that the answer came from the filing rather than from a model's memory.

The hard requirement was coverage plus grounding: answers for any company on the NYSE or NASDAQ, each traceable back to the filing it came from.

What I Built

  • Ingestion pipeline

    Python services pulling filings from EDGAR SEC, then normalizing and chunking them for retrieval — over 100,000 documents covering the full NYSE and NASDAQ listings.

  • AWS RAG infrastructure

    Architected the indexing and retrieval layer, supporting 1,000+ indexed documents with query performance that stayed stable as the corpus grew.

  • REST API layer

    Part of the 20+ REST APIs I built in Flask and Node.js, integrating EDGAR SEC, OpenAI, Salesforce, and Google Sheets.

  • Cross-platform delivery

    A React front end shipped as a PWA plus iOS and Google Play builds, part of cross-platform work reaching over 10,000 users.

Engineering Decisions

Decisions made and the reasoning behind each
Decision Reasoning
Retrieval over fine-tuning Filings arrive continuously and answers must cite a source. RAG keeps the corpus current without retraining and keeps every answer traceable to a document.
Preprocess the full corpus up front Parsing EDGAR filings at query time would have made responses unpredictably slow. Normalizing ahead of time moved the cost off the request path.
AWS-managed indexing A small team could not operate self-hosted vector infrastructure alongside everything else in flight.
PWA first, native wrappers second One codebase covered web, iOS, and Android, matching the team's capacity for maintenance.

What I Took From It

RAG systems fail quietly. When retrieval returns the wrong chunk, the model still writes a fluent, confident answer — nothing crashes and no error surfaces. Spot-checking finds the obvious breakages and misses the rest, which is what pushed me toward treating evaluation as real test infrastructure rather than manual review.

That thread runs directly into my current work on LLM evaluation and MCP-based testing, where the question is not whether a system produced an answer but whether it produced the right one, repeatedly, under change.
