Privacy Policy for Contract King

Effective Date: August 20, 2026

Overview

Contract King is a Chrome extension that finds the terms of service and privacy policies on a site, then explains in plain language what you are agreeing to. It reads those documents so you do not have to, grading each one and flagging clauses such as mandatory arbitration, sale of personal data, AI training on your content, indefinite retention, and automatic renewal.

In short: there are no accounts and no tracking. Contract King analyzes public legal documents, not you. The only content that leaves your browser is the text of a terms or privacy page, sent for analysis when you ask for one.

Data Collection and Usage

  • No account, no identity. Contract King does not ask you to sign up or log in. It does not create a user id, and it does not collect your name, email address, or any personal information.
  • No tracking or analytics. The extension contains no analytics, telemetry, or advertising code, and it does not build a profile of you or record your browsing history.
  • Local storage. Settings, scan state, and the list of sites already known to have an analysis are kept on your device with chrome.storage.local. Uninstalling the extension removes them.
  • Document text. When you analyze a document, the extracted text of that legal page is sent to the Contract King service so a model can read it. This is the public terms or privacy page of a website — not your personal data, and not the content of other pages you visit.

Permissions

Chrome asks you to approve the following. Each is used only for the purpose described here.

Permission Why it is needed
storage Saves your settings and scan results locally on your device.
activeTab and tabs Reads the address of the page you are on so the extension can tell whether a saved analysis exists for it, and so the toolbar badge can reflect the current site.
Access to sites you visit A content script scores the links on a page to locate its terms, privacy policy, cookie notice, or license, and extracts the document text when you choose to analyze one. Detection is plain rules running locally; the page text is only sent onward when you request an analysis.

Text is extracted from the page already open in your browser rather than refetched by the server, because a server-side fetch of a legal page usually returns a consent wall or a bot check instead of the document.

How Analysis Works

Analysis runs on Cloudflare, using Workers AI for inference. A submitted document is chunked and checked against a fixed taxonomy of clause categories; every finding has to quote the passage it came from, and anything ungrounded or off-taxonomy is discarded. Documents are hashed, so a policy that has not changed returns the stored analysis instead of being processed again.

Stored alongside each analysis are the document's address, title, and text, its findings, and a snapshot used to show you what changed when a policy is revised. These records describe public legal documents and are not linked to any individual user.

Data Sharing

Contract King does not sell, rent, or trade any data. Document text is processed by Cloudflare Workers AI purely to produce the analysis you asked for. There are no advertising networks, data brokers, or third-party trackers involved.

Security

Requests to the service travel over HTTPS, and the API restricts which extension origins may call it. Because the extension holds no account credentials and stores no personal information, there is no login or payment data to expose.

Site Indexing

So that an analysis can be ready the moment you land on a site, Contract King also indexes legal documents from public websites ahead of time. This crawler fetches only publicly available pages, and it honors robots.txt for every host — including Crawl-delay — making one request at a time per site and identifying itself with a contact address. Sites that disallow crawling are skipped.

Open Source

Contract King is released into the public domain, so every claim on this page can be checked against the source: the detection rules, the clause taxonomy, exactly what the extension sends, and what the service stores. An extension that tells you what you are agreeing to should be willing to show you the same.

Not Legal Advice

Contract King summarizes documents using an automated model. It can miss things and it can be wrong. Its output is meant to help you decide what deserves a closer look — it is not legal advice, and it is no substitute for reading a contract that matters or consulting a qualified attorney.
